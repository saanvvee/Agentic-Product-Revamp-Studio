## Aim of the Course

The course aims to equip students with the knowledge and skills needed to design, build, and deploy intelligent multi-agent workflows powered by Large Language Models (LLMs). Students will learn to harness the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to automate complex, context-aware tasks using advanced agentic design patterns. Emphasis is placed on real-world application, secure deployment, and data privacy, including the use of local LLMs. By the end of the course, students will be prepared to develop robust, scalable AI-driven task automation systems across diverse domains.

---

## Course Overview and Context

"Agentic AI" is an advanced undergraduate course designed to explore the intersection of artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and task automation. The course spans over 16 weeks and provides learners with the knowledge and practical skills required to build intelligent multi-agent systems powered by Large Language Models (LLMs).

Emphasizing task automation through structured conversational workflows, the course begins with the review of foundational concepts such as transformer architecture, embeddings, LLM training, etc. Further, learners will explore agentic design patterns including planning, reasoning, tool use, and self-reflection, and gain hands-on experience with leading frameworks such as *CrewAI* and Google's *Agent Development Kit (ADK)*. The course also covers emerging communication protocols like Anthropic's *Model Context Protocol (MCP)* and Google's *Agent2Agent (A2A)*, with a strong focus on secure, responsible deployment using monitoring, observability, and guardrails.

By the end of the course, participants will be able to design and deploy multi-modal agents across domains such as customer support, healthcare, and automated software development.

## Course Philosophy

This course will be delivered using a **Project-Based Learning (PjBL)** approach, where students learn Agentic AI related concepts by designing and developing AI agents to solve complex real-world problems. Students will progressively build an agentic solution, applying concepts of:

- Generative AI using LLM acting as the reasoning layer for Agentic AI
- Frameworks to build workflows: CrewAI and ADK
- Multi-agent Orchestration and Planning
- Safety and Guardrails
- Agent Evaluation for performance improvement
- Deployment and Observability

## Additional Guidelines for Team Responsibility

- All students are expected to contribute across core components (Agents, Tools, Orchestrator, etc. and integrations). While tasks may be divided within teams, **roles must rotate across phases**.
- **No student may limit their contribution to a single area** (e.g., only tool design, only UI or documentation). Each student is expected to make significant technical contributions to the success of the project.
- During evaluation (demo/viva), **each student should be able to explain and modify any part of the project**.
- Individual performance will be assessed based on demonstrated understanding of all components.
- All evaluations will be based on the work presented by the students as well as the questions asked, or the problems given to implement.
- **Cases of AI-generated code or plagiarism will be taken seriously and reported according to the university's policy on Unfair Means (UFM).** It is essential that all work is original and adheres to academic integrity.
- There is a **mandatory requirement to upload the project to a public repository on GitHub**.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy
Students are expected to attend classes regularly. Failure to follow the classes regularly and adhere to the expected attendance percentage will result in losing quiz/lab marks and a reduction of the grade as per the University's grading policy.

### Recourse Examination Policy
This is an applied course focused on real-world problem-solving using Agentic AI. **There will be no recourse examination held.** The student can only repeat the course when it is offered in the subsequent semester/year.

### Make-up Policy
Make-up will be offered **only for the Quiz component** to a student who has provided prior intimation of absence before the conduct of the exam. There will be no make-up provided for the rest of the assessment components.

### Behavior Expectations
No mobile phones and other distractive gadgets are permitted in the class.

### Academic Dishonesty / Cheating / Plagiarism
Plagiarism and dishonesty in any form in any evaluation component will lead to appropriate disciplinary action.
